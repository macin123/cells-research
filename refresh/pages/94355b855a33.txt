Biosketch
My laboratory investigates aberrant cell signaling mechanisms that give rise to Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy (ACM), an inherited heart disease characterized by early onset of arrhythmias, cardiac dysfunction, and increased risk of sudden cardiac death. Often referred to as "Sudden Cardiac Death in Young Athletes," patients with ACM display progressive myocardial injury, such as cell death, myocardial inflammation, and fibrofatty infiltration of the heart. All these clinical and pathological phenotypes ultimately lead to heart failure and increased mortality in patients with ACM. In addition to studying abnormal cell signaling pathways, my laboratory tests the efficacy of small molecule inhibitors, repurposed FDA approved drugs, and up-n-coming drugs for potential clinical utility in animal models of ACM. My laboratory performs a variety of functional studies, such as cardiac function via dopplar echocardiography and electrocardiography (ECGs), and pathological studies, such as immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence microscopy. I work with a phenomenal team of collaborators, at FSU, nationally, and abroad. My research is funded by foundational, federal, and pharmaceutical industries.

Research Focus
My primary research focus utilizes in vivo models of Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy (ACM) in order to elucidate aberrant cell signaling mechanisms that give rise to ACM functional and pathological phenotypes. I am a translational research physiologist with extensive experience (>15yrs) testing the efficaciousness of small molecule inhibitors, repurposed FDA-approved drugs, and monoclonal antibodies on cardiovascular functional outcomes (e.g. echocardiography, ECG telemetry, blood pressure, and vascular contractile properties) and the prevention of myocardial injury (e.g., inflammation, cell death, fibrosis). I additionally conduct research investigating the impact of exercise on reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and myocardial antioxidant capacity in ACM. I am pleased to be part of a collaborative network of clinical and basic researchers performing translational studies with murine and cellular models of ACM, internally, externally, and abroad.
