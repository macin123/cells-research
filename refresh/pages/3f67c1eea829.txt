Project Description
Why do some armed conflicts experience widespread sexual violence while others do not? This project examines variation in conflict-related sexual violence across civil wars, focusing on how armed-group characteristics, gender norms, organizational practices, and conflict dynamics may shape the use or restraint of sexual violence. The broader goal is to better understand why violence becomes sexualized in some conflicts but not in others.
Research Tasks: The research assistant will assist with literature review, document collection, and data collection on armed groups and civil conflicts. Tasks will include reviewing academic and policy sources, identifying relevant case materials, coding armed-group and conflict characteristics, organizing sources, and assisting with the construction and validation of a cross-national dataset.
Skills that research assistant(s) may need: Required: Strong reading, writing, organization, and attention to detail; ability to work independently and engage professionally with research on sensitive topics.
Recommended: Interest or coursework in political science, international affairs, conflict studies, human rights, or gender; familiarity with Excel/Google Sheets, literature reviews, or basic data analysis. No prior research or coding experience is required.
Mentoring Philosophy
My mentoring philosophy emphasizes creating a collaborative and supportive environment in which students can develop practical research skills while confidence in their research and communication skills. I plan to provide regular feedback and guidance while gradually encouraging greater independence as the student becomes comfortable with the research process. I hope to learn about the student’s academic and professional goals and tailor opportunities accordingly. Ultimately, my goal is for the student to leave the project with stronger research skills, confidence working with academic research, and a meaningful understanding of how political science research develops from an initial question to an empirical study.
