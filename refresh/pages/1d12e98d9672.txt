Biosketch
With a PhD in Chemistry and Environmental Science from Rutgers University and New Jersey Institute of Technology, and extensive postdoctoral training at Texas A&M University, I have dedicated my career to elucidating the molecular mechanisms of one-carbon metabolism and its role in preventing metabolic disorders like peripheral neuropathy and neural tube defects. My research integrates mouse model studies, including embryonic, electrophysiology, and mammalian cell culture studies. Further, incorporating essential techniques as quantitative, RT-qPCR, histology, and IHC staining, to bridge nutritional interventions with clinical outcomes.
My work has produced high-impact publications, such as the PNAS study demonstrating how dietary folic acid prevents peripheral neuropathy in mouse models of neural tube defects and type 2 diabetes, published January 2026. Earlier contributions include engineering the 2-oxoglutarate dehydrogenase complex for altered substrate recognition (Reactions, 2022) and nanocarbon membranes for pathogen-free water purification (Separation and Purification Technology, 2021). These efforts, complemented by invited talks at FASEB conferences and awards like the Emerging Scholar at the 2024 Precision Nutrition conference, underscore my commitment to precision nutrition and protein engineering.
As an instructor for General Chemistry courses at NJIT, including tailored labs for engineering students, and a mentor to
undergraduates now at Harvard and Cornell, I foster inclusive STEM education, particularly for underrepresented groups through programs like Educational Opportunity Program at New Jersey Institute of Technology. My service as a faculty interviewer for FSU College of Medicine admissions and editorial roles for journals like Cancer Medicine further demonstrate my dedication to advancing biomedical research communities.
Currently as Research Faculty at FSU College of Medicine, I aim to expand investigations into one-carbon metabolism's
neuroprotective roles, tracking nutritional biomarkers at a cellular level, leveraging my skills in both cell culture and animal model studies combined with high-throughput assays and finally translate findings into public health strategies. This trajectory positions me to lead innovative, interdisciplinary projects addressing metabolic diseases through nutrition and molecular biology.
Memberships
1) American Association for the Advancement of Science
2) American Society of Nutrition
Research Focus
Current focus of my lab is to study complex human diseases using mammalian and mouse models. In addition to understanding disease mechanisms, our group also investigates on dietary intervention approaches to rescue or prevent the occurence and progression of the disease.
