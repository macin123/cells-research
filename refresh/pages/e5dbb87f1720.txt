Project Description
"Mapping Out the Face of Christ in Baroque Italy Spain" aims to build a database of all the depictions of Christ's face in painting, print, drawing, and sculpture executed in Italy and Spain during the period 1580–1660. The overall objective of this research is to establish a chart of typologies necessary to 1) determine the impact of Counter-Reformation archaeological and historical studies devoted to the human appearance of Christ and its specificities on late sixteenth- and seventeenth-century artists; 2) identify the reasons for possible divergences between the Counter-Reformation's instructions in matter of sacred iconography and the practice and conventions of artists; 3) circumscribe the role and strength of traditional iconography in conveying depictions of Christ at odds with historical and archaeological knowledge promoted by the Counter-Reformation; 4) assess the role of the ancient canon of beauty in reinforcing or disrupting the Christian aesthetics of divinity; 5) determine the independent role of artists in reconfiguring the depiction of Christ in accordance with specific notions of devotion or beauty. This research project is destined to 1) the development and expansion of undergraduate and graduate courses in early modern art; 2) the development of a major monograph on the body in the baroque age. 
Research Tasks: The student selected for this project will 1) build a database in conformity with the criteria discussed in advance with the mentor 2) identify typologies as the project advances and create a chart accordingly (data analysis) 3) gather the relevant literature review in relation to the typologies identified and/or proposed.
Skills that research assistant(s) may need: Required: a basic knowledge of early modern art history; critical aptitude; analytical mindset; strong taste for research rooted in curiosity; mental flexibility. 
Recommended: basic knowledge of either Italian or Spanish.
Mentoring Philosophy
I love research: it is both a profession and a passion. I also consider myself a humanist and a historian. As a humanist, I intend to create a relationship with the mentee based on dialogue and respectful discussion. As a historian, I value accuracy and subtlety immensely, and I also cultivate a sense of distance (the discipline of reining in our natural tendency to interpret facts subjectively and in support of an ideology and/or pre-established notion). I want the mentee to acquire all the necessary tools for research step by step, joyously if possible, engaging with the research in a personal manner. When the mentorship works, mentees not only assist the mentor; they start thinking how they could independently develop their own research. I would like to inspire my mentees to undertake a graduate degree and use the material they gathered as a catalyst for their future research projects.
