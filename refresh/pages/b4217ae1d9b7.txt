Project Description
This fall, Dr. Stenson is programming concert repertoire around Half the Sky by Nicholas D. Kristof and Sheryl WuDunn. The book—and its accompanying PBS documentary—explores global issues affecting women, including sex trafficking, maternal mortality, and gender-based violence, while highlighting pathways for change through women’s economic empowerment. Throughout the semester, the FSU ensemble will engage with selected readings and documentary excerpts alongside the musical repertoire.
Dr. Wright-Cleveland will direct this research project to augment that performance. Undergraduate researchers may work toward one of three outcomes: 
1. research and write an essay about one of the global issues affecting women that is explored in this composition; 
2. research local agencies engaged in supporting women and build a resource fair to be held in conjunction with the concert on November 3;
3. research and prepare a poster to be published in conjunction with a panel discussion about this performance during FSU's Festival of the Creative Arts in February 2027. 
Research Tasks: Literature review of Half the Sky by Nicholas D. Kristof and Sheryl WuDunn; data collection on global issues affecting women; survey construction about impact of Half the Sky Project; conducting interviews; writing analysis of data and literature; writing a documentary.
Skills that research assistant(s) may need: There are no specialized skills required for this project. Curiosity about connecting ideas across disciplines - what we call a cultural studies approach - is of great benefit. 
Mentoring Philosophy
I mentor UROP students by giving mentees’ ownership of their work and promoting accountability. Under my mentorship, each undergraduate researcher should experience new ways to connect ideas and develop inquiry. In other words, mentees are expected to ask questions and build on their answers while consulting experts. When I cannot be an expert, I help mentees find the right expert. 
Additional Information
This is a first-time collaborative effort and we will be developing possibilities as we go. If you like to try new things and want to have an impact on our local community, talk with me about your ideas for this project.
