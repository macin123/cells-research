Research and Professional Interests:
Organismal longevity begins at the cellular level. Somatic cells, which constitute the body's tissues and organs, have a limited replicative lifespan. This means they can only divide a certain number of times before they cease functioning properly. On the other hand, germ cells, which produce eggs and sperm, have an evolutionary perspective of living forever. This is because they are responsible for transmitting genetic information from one generation to the next.
How germ cells self-renew remains an enigma. Recent findings have revealed that the quality control of the nuclear envelope during gametogenesis, commonly known as meiosis, plays a pivotal role in determining the replicative lifespan of cells. Our current research aims to establish proof of concept that meiosis in the budding yeast, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, serves as an effective system for determining the molecular mechanisms underlying cellular rejuvenation.
