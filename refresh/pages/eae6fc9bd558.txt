Project Description
The Florida’s Linked Opioid Response and Epidemiological Network for Comprehensive Evaluation (FLORENCE) Project examines the prevalence of opioid use behaviors and opioid use disorder (OUD) among Floridians to inform the state’s opioid abatement strategies.
The project integrates primary and secondary data sources. Primary data collection centers on the design, implementation, and analysis of a targeted survey assessing substance use behaviors, treatment access, service utilization, and related outcomes among Floridians with a history of opioid use disorder and incarceration. Secondary analyses draw on representative surveys and Medicaid claims data to estimate the prevalence of opioid misuse and OUD and to evaluate the reach and effectiveness of evidence-based treatments. Findings are intended to ultimately contribute to measurable reductions in opioid-related harm across Florida.
Students will contribute to the project by supporting the design, implementation, and data management of a primary survey assessing substance use behaviors, treatment access, and service outcomes among Floridians with histories of opioid use disorder and incarceration, as well as assisting with secondary analyses of representative surveys and Medicaid claims data.
Research Tasks: literature review, survey design and development, primary data collection, data management and cleaning, quantitative data analysis (primary survey and secondary sources).
Skills that research assistant(s) may need: Recommended skills: Literature searching, survey methodology and questionnaire design, data collection, data cleaning, quantitative statistical analysis and interpretation
Mentoring Philosophy
Effective mentorship begins with taking the time to understand each student’s individual goals, motivations, and existing strengths. Through careful evaluation, I work to build on those foundations rather than impose a one-size-fits-all approach. I believe the strongest mentoring relationships are grounded in mutual respect, open communication, and a genuine interest in the mentee’s development. I strive to create an interactive and supportive learning environment in which students feel safe to ask questions, take on leadership roles, and learn from mistakes without fear of judgment. By sharing relevant experiences from my own path and encouraging inquiry-driven exploration, I help students connect theory with practice. At the same time, I give students meaningful ownership of their work and foster development by balancing encouragement and belief in their potential with opportunities to conduct independent, student-led research. In doing so, I aim to help mentees develop both the skills and the confidence to become independent, thoughtful researchers and professionals.
