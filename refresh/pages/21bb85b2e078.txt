Anthony Ruberto Ph.D.

anthony.ruberto@med.fsu.edu 
Main Campus 
Biosketch
Dr. Anthony Ruberto is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Biomedical Sciences at the Florida State University College of Medicine. The Ruberto Laboratory investigates parasites and free-living amoebae that cause or contribute to significant human disease, with a main focus on malaria-causing Plasmodium parasites and primary amebic meningoencephalitis-causing Naegleria fowleri . The laboratory seeks to identify new treatments for these infections and to understand the cellular and molecular mechanisms that allow pathogens to survive drug treatment and develop resistance.
Dr. Ruberto earned his Ph.D. under the mentorship of Dr. Mich&egrave;le Teboul at the Institute of Biology Valrose, Universit&eacute; C&ocirc;te d'Azur, in Nice, France. His doctoral research examined the relationship between circadian rhythms and metabolic dysfunction in the liver.
For his postdoctoral training, Dr. Ruberto joined the laboratory of Dr. Ivo Mueller in the Department of Insect Vectors and Parasites at the Institut Pasteur in Paris, France, where he investigated the biology of malaria parasites within the mosquito. This work later brought him to the Institut Pasteur du Cambodge in Cambodia, a malaria-endemic country, where he applied his expertise in cellular and molecular biology to investigate malaria parasite within the human, including dormant liver-stage forms that can persist and later activate to cause relapsing malaria.
Dr. Ruberto subsequently joined the Center for Tropical and Emerging Global Diseases at the University of Georgia, where he trained under the direction of Dr. Dennis Kyle in antiparasitic drug discovery and mechanisms of drug resistance. During this period, his research encompassed several medically important pathogens, including Plasmodium species, Naegleria fowleri , Trypanosoma cruzi , and Toxoplasma gondii . In 2024, he received an NIH National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases K99/R00 Pathway to Independence Award to advance his research program in infectious-disease drug discovery and resistance.
In May 2026, Dr. Ruberto joined the Florida State University College of Medicine and established the Ruberto Laboratory in the Department of Biomedical Sciences.

Research Focus
The Ruberto Laboratory studies parasitic pathogens and free-living amoebae that cause significant human disease, with a primary focus on malaria-causing Plasmodium parasites and Naegleria fowleri . The laboratory seeks to identify new therapeutic strategies and understand the biological mechanisms that enable these pathogens to survive treatment, persist within the host, and develop drug resistance.
Current research integrates cellular and molecular biology, drug-discovery approaches, functional genomics, and advanced imaging to investigate parasite development, treatment response, and drug resistance. Particular areas of interest include malaria parasite development in the liver and blood, dormant liver-stage forms associated with relapsing malaria, and mechanisms of resistance to antimalarial compounds. The laboratory also investigates therapeutic vulnerabilities in N. fowleri and other medically important parasites.
By combining fundamental studies of parasite biology with translational drug-discovery research, the Ruberto Laboratory aims to uncover new therapeutic targets and advance the development of more effective treatments for diseases caused by medically important eukaryotic pathogens.
