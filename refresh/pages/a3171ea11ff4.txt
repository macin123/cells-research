Project Description
To map and analyze existing qualitative data in a systematic research synthesis of the perceived risk-related needs of juvenile offenders transitioning from correctional facilities into the wider community and to gain insight into their lived experiences. The aim is to eventually conduct qualitative, semi-structured interviews with juvenile offenders (ages 12-18) from the US and the Caribbean region (specifically focusing on sites with high disproportionality). In our data collection, we will focus on their personal perspectives on the drivers of their recidivism, including family dynamics, school disengagement, community violence, peer pressure, and lack of other support services.
Research Tasks: Mainly data collection, data analysis, supporting tasks concerning IRB Submission.

Mentoring Philosophy
My mentoring approach centers on a strong partnership built on mutual respect, honesty, and integrity. I set an example by leading proactively and supporting my mentee through each challenge, fostering mutual development and trust. This active engagement creates a safe environment where vulnerability becomes an opportunity for learning, and failures become steps toward growth. By understanding their individual goals, I customize my advice to help them reach their full potential and build lasting professional confidence.
Core Pillars:
• Mutual Trust: Clear communication keeps our relationship honest and transparent.
• Shared Growth: We grow together by learning from each success and setback.
• Actionable Insight: Practical tips turn ideas into real, tangible results.
Ultimately, I treat others the way I want to be treated, recognizing that this is a learning process I am also part of. I feel privileged to support and mentor someone at a different stage in their life.
