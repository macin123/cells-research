Project Description
This project explores how organizations’ AI adoption and AI-related policies may attract or discourage young talent in the service industry. As companies increasingly use AI in areas such as customer service, operations, and employee management, they are also communicating their AI strategies to current and potential employees. For young workers, AI may signal that an organization is innovative, modern, and prepared for the future, but it may also raise concerns about job security, workplace monitoring, reduced human interaction, or the replacement of employees. We are particularly interested in how organizations present their use of AI and whether their AI-related claims reflect their actual practices, an issue connected to the emerging concept of AI washing. Undergraduate research assistants will help examine company websites, career pages, job advertisements, and other publicly available information to understand how service organizations communicate about AI and how these strategies may shape their attractiveness to young talent.

Mentoring Philosophy
My mentoring philosophy is centered on helping students develop strong research skills, critical thinking, and confidence as future leaders. I believe effective mentoring should be built on mutual respect, trust, and open communication while encouraging students to take ownership of their learning. I strive to create a safe and supportive environment where students understand that making mistakes is a normal and valuable part of the learning process. Rather than simply providing answers, I encourage students to ask questions, explore ideas, and learn through inquiry and hands-on experience. I also believe in balancing knowledge with action and experience, giving students opportunities to apply what they learn to real-world situations. At the same time, I encourage students to step outside their comfort zones and view challenges as opportunities for growth. My goal is not only to help students become better researchers, but also to develop their critical thinking, confidence, curiosity, and ability to make thoughtful decisions—qualities that will help them become capable and responsible future leaders.
